Your round trip

  1. A folder is assigned to you. It appears in your queue marked Assigned, and the bell in the top bar tells you it arrived. Nothing is assigned to you silently.
  2. Work through its documents. Open each one to see its page image beside its editable fields. Correct anything the draft got wrong, then Accept it, or leave a comment on it if it needs someone else’s eyes. Save Draft keeps your edits without deciding. The progress bar on the folder counts accepted documents, so you can always see how much is left.
  3. Leave a comment where the problem is. Each document has its own comment thread, behind the Comments button beside the status pill. Use it for “the date here is ambiguous” rather than putting the note on the whole folder. This is how a document is marked as needing another look: the folder’s document list shows a comment icon on every row, carrying the number of comments, and clicking it reads the thread without opening the document. The Commented filter narrows the folder to the documents with a question still open on them. Either of you can mark a comment resolved.
  4. Submit the folder. The Submit for review button hands it to the administrators and moves it to Submitted. You can add a note saying what you were unsure about. Submit only unlocks once every document in the folder has been reviewed — otherwise “submitted” would just mean you had pressed a button, and whoever opened it next would find unfinished work.
  5. It comes back, or it is approved. If an administrator sends it back it returns as Returned with their note, and it is yours to edit again. Once they have accepted every document it becomes Approved, your progress bar turns green, and it is locked — approved work can no longer be changed without reopening it.

Editing the transcription

  • Paragraph indents. The draft indents the paragraphs the page indents. Where it gets one wrong, put the cursor in the paragraph and press Tab to indent it or Shift+Tab to take the indent away. Esc returns Tab to moving between fields.
  • Multi-page documents. Each scan after the first begins with a [page 2] marker, drawn as a rule across the transcription. Every scan is transcribed, including a handwritten draft or a carbon copy of a page already there — two versions of one letter are two pages, not a repeat to skip.
  • Your edits are saved and backed up. Accept or Save Draft writes the whole record — transcription, title, date, author, recipient, place, your notes — and the next backup carries that version, replacing the one before it.

Your account

Click your name in the top bar. You can change your password, or close your account — which signs you out for good and ends your access. Closing it does not erase your work: the changelog still records what you transcribed and submitted.

Reading the changelog

Every folder carries its own history — who was assigned it, who submitted it, who sent it back and why, who approved it. It reads as plain sentences (“completed by John, reviewed by Paul”), so months later it is still obvious who did what.

You see everything — every folder, every reviewer, every document — and you are the only role that can approve work or export it. Your job here is to hand work out, check it when it comes back, and release it.

The loop you run

  1. Assign a folder. In Team, pick a folder and a reviewer. The folder list is a dropdown of real folder IDs rather than a text box — typing a folder’s name where its ID belongs used to produce an assignment that pointed at nothing, leaving the reviewer with an empty queue and no error. The reviewer is notified.
  2. Watch progress without interrupting. Each folder card shows a progress bar computed from its documents, so you can tell “started” from “nearly done” at a glance. Open comments identify documents that still need attention. The Contributors table in Team totals each reviewer’s folders, documents, submissions, times sent back and average turnaround.
  3. Review a submission. A submitted folder arrives with the reviewer’s note. Open its documents, read the comment threads — the Comments button beside the status pill carries a count when any are open — and use History in that panel to see exactly which fields were changed and by whom.
  4. Send it back, or finish reviewing it. Send back puts it back in the reviewer’s hands with your note attached — say what needs fixing, since that note is the whole message. There is no Approve button: accepting the last document approves the folder and marks it Ready to export. Unreview one and the approval is withdrawn again, so unfinished work cannot reach the deliverable — which is the one thing review exists to prevent.
  5. Export. Export is per folder and administrator-only: a ZIP holding the folder’s CSV and every exported page image. Reviewers can reach neither the button nor the endpoint behind it.

Managing people

  • Adding people. In Team → People, create an account with a temporary password, or make a join code in Team → Invite and hand it out: whoever holds it creates their own account from the sign-in page. Administrators add reviewers; only the owner adds administrators.
  • Roster CSV. Download who holds which folder, with status and last activity, from Team.
  • Reassignment. Assigning a folder to a different reviewer moves it; the changelog keeps both names, so the earlier reviewer’s work is still attributed to them.

Reading the Contributors table

  • Last active is the last time that person did anything — editing or accepting a document counts, not only submitting a folder. Someone part way through a folder shows today’s date, not “never”.
  • Avg turnaround is the hours from being given a folder to first submitting it, averaged over their folders. It stays blank until they have submitted something, and it measures the first submission, so work sent back and corrected does not read as slowness — Sent back is where that shows.

Accounts and backups

  • Anyone can close their own account from their name in the top bar. It ends their access and their sessions; it does not remove their history, so the changelog still says who did the work. The owner cannot close their account; they transfer ownership first, so the site is never left without one.
  • Backups run inside this server, every ten minutes, and only when something has changed. There is no separate scheduled task to check. Each pass snapshots the database and refreshes the documents’ own folders, replacing the file for a document that has been edited rather than stacking up copies of it.

Administrator-only, enforced on the server

Approving, returning, exporting, deleting, editing pipeline settings, the roster and the deletion log are all refused for a reviewer account by the server itself — not merely hidden in the interface. Hiding a button stops the button; it does not stop anyone who knows the address behind it.

Scans go in as a folder; the model proposes where each document starts, drafts the transcription and the catalogue fields; people review; the folder goes out as a CSV, JSON records and images.

If you ever need to correct the Folder ID afterward on the Confirm Split screen below, an optional Folder Name field there lets you set (or fix) the full label too.
  2. Confirm the split. The model proposes where each document starts, and you check its work one document at a time. The viewer shows the document's scans — page through them with the arrows (or the ← → keys) — then click Looks right to confirm it and move to the next, or Needs fixing if the boundaries are wrong.
    "Needs fixing" offers the only two things that can be wrong: the document ends too late (click Split here on the page that really begins the next document) or it ends too early (click Merge with next listed document). You can also tick any two or more cards in the document list and click Merge selected, even when the documents are not beside each other. Drag cards to reorder them; their positional Document IDs update to match the new order.
    Each document's ID is pre-filled (E2241AAA, E2241AAB, …) and editable. Confirming auto-drafts the first batch. Got a boundary wrong and only noticed later? That's fixable after drafting too — see "Fixing a split you already confirmed" below.
  3. Draft Pending. A large split drafts one batch per click. Documents awaiting drafting do not appear in the review queue yet — that's the stage most reviewers get surprised by. Keep clicking Draft Pending until the folder's "awaiting drafting" count reaches zero.
  4. Review & approve each drafted document (below).
  5. Export the folder (below).

Browsing folders

The New Documents queue opens on a grid of folder cards — one per archival folder — plus any batches still awaiting split confirmation. Click a folder to see its documents; use ← All folders to come back. Each card reads:

  • ready to review — drafted documents you can open now.
  • awaiting drafting — still need Draft Pending; not listed yet.
  • N of M reviewed — the bar turns green and the card says Ready to export once every document carries a reviewer’s verdict. A folder still out with a reviewer shows their progress instead, in pale blue, and never green.

Reviewing a document

Open a document to see its page image beside its editable fields. Every AI-produced field can be corrected. New Documents specifically let you edit two things the other workflow can't:

  • Title. Auto-built from Document Type / Author / Recipient / Date and normally updates as you edit those. If it still reads wrong, type your own — it's saved as an override and travels into the exported CSV's Title column.
  • Document ID. Use Edit ID to fix a typo or a wrong split-time suggestion; the change follows the document everywhere, including export.

Then set a status: Accept (approved, as-is or edited), or Save Draft to keep edits without changing status. Accept is reversible with Mark Unreviewed. A document needing follow-up gets a comment, which the folder’s document list then shows on its row.

Fixing a split you already confirmed

Bad boundaries often only become obvious once you're reading the drafts — two documents that are really one, or one that's really two. Open the folder and click Fix Split, then tick the documents that are wrong. Choose Merge into One to join two or more existing drafts, or Re-cut Boundaries to return their scans to the split viewer.

Documents merged into one may be non-adjacent, but must come from the same upload. A merge keeps and joins their reviewed transcriptions in scan order, asks the model to regenerate the combined metadata/title, and returns the result to Unreviewed. Re-cutting replaces the selected documents and re-drafts their changed pages. Documents you did not select remain separate.

Exporting a completed folder

After every document has been accepted by both the reviewer and an admin, the completion prompt offers Export or Not yet. Exports are saved under data/exports on the host and copied to both backup destinations. No files are downloaded to your device. You can use Export to host later after choosing Not yet.

What costs money

Uploading, browsing, reviewing, and exporting are all free. Splitting and drafting both make real model calls — splitting asks where each document begins, drafting produces the transcription and the catalogue record. Each is checked against the Max total cost setting before it runs — once all-time spend hits the cap, drafting stops on its own. Adjust the cap and per-click batch sizes on the Settings tab.

Working across devices

The site runs on its own server and behaves the same from every device:

  • Uploads from any device land in the site's storage, never on your laptop or phone.
  • Exports are built on the server, where an admin downloads them.
  • Edits (titles, IDs, review decisions) are saved instantly, so everyone sees the same state.
